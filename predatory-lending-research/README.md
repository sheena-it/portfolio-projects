# 📑 Predatory Lending and Financial Vulnerability: A Bibliometric Review

**A published bibliometric research study mapping global academic trends in predatory lending and financial vulnerability, using Scopus data, R (Bibliometrix), and VOSviewer.**

📰 **Published in:** *International Journal of Multidisciplinary: Applied Business and Education Research (IJMABER)*, Vol. 7, No. 6, 2026
🔗 DOI: [10.11594/ijmaber.07.06.04](https://doi.org/10.11594/ijmaber.07.06.04)
👥 Co-authored with Angel Jade V. Frialde, Evette Jillian R. Maiquez, and Jennifer P. Solis (Bulacan State University)

---

## 📌 What This Study Does

Predatory lending — loans with hidden fees, deceptive terms, or exploitative interest rates — is a global financial harm that spans economics, law, and social science research. This study asks: **how has academic research on predatory lending evolved, and where are the gaps?**

Rather than reviewing papers manually, we used bibliometric analysis to map the entire research landscape quantitatively: who's publishing, where, on what themes, and how those themes are shifting over time.

## 🔬 Methodology

- **Data source:** Scopus database, exported November 5, 2025
- **Search strategy:** Boolean query combining "predatory lending," "subprime lending," and "microfinance" with "financial vulnerability," "debt trap," "consumer protection," and "household debt" — limited to English-language sources in Economics, Business, Social Science, and Decision Science
- **Data cleaning:** 402 records retrieved → 6 non-English removed → 9 duplicates removed → 46 records with missing abstracts removed → **341 records** in the final dataset
- **Analysis tools:** Bibliometrix (R, v4.1.4) and VOSviewer (v1.6.20)

My contributions: **conceptualization, investigation, visualization, and writing** (original draft + review & editing).

## 📊 Key Findings

- **The United States dominates the field** with 369 published studies — largely driven by research into the 2008 housing crisis and subprime lending
- **Indonesia is the leading emerging research producer** (49 studies), reflecting growing academic attention on digital lending and consumer protection in Southeast Asia
- Core research is concentrated in just three journals — *Housing Policy Debate*, *Business Lawyer*, and *Banking Law Journal* — confirming predatory lending is studied primarily as a legal/policy issue, not purely a financial one
- Keyword analysis shows a **thematic shift**: research is moving from traditional mortgage/subprime lending toward **microfinance, poverty, and financial exclusion** — signaling growing academic interest in how predatory lending affects developing nations
- **Research gap identified:** despite the rapid growth of digital lending apps across Asia and Africa (including the Philippines), most existing research still centers on developed, Western markets

## 💡 Why It Matters

This gap is the paper's central contribution: as online lending platforms expand rapidly in developing countries, academic research hasn't kept pace. Our findings support the case for expanded borrower protections and more research specifically on digital lending in developing-nation contexts — including the Philippines, where the SEC has already issued cease-and-desist orders against online lending apps for abusive collection practices and data privacy violations.
